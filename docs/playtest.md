# Start here: your first college career

This is the short guide for Danny's first college play-through (the college launch, Wed Nov 25, 2026). It replaces the
old NBA play-test guide (v0.112.1). Notes go in the play-test notebook:
https://claude.ai/artifact/9MjakW3Aqhy7hqiZgrHF3r. The next build run reads them first.

## Before you start
- Download the newest release zip from the GitHub releases page (`HardwoodManager-vX.Y.Z-win-x64.zip`), unzip it
  anywhere and run `HardwoodManager.exe`. Any later release loads your save, so you can update whenever a new one lands.
- Your graphics packs (`prographics`, `collegegraphics` in `Desktop\FMNEWGANv2`) load at start as before.
- Saves live in `Documents\Hardwood Manager`. Keep the file: every later build is tested against a launch-build save.

## Starting the career (two minutes)
1. On the start screen, set **League** (top right) to **College basketball**.
2. Pick your program in the list. Blue bloods (Duke, North Carolina, Kentucky) start near the top; a mid-major is a
   rebuild with a patient AD.
3. Fill in your coach on the left (name, age, background, licence). Leave the seed as it is or press Random.
4. Press **Start career with selected team**.

## What to expect in the first hour
- **The Welcome** is the first inbox message: your program, the AD, the season goal, your rival, the first game and how
  recruiting works. The **assistant** panel on the right lists what needs you before you play on.
- **Recruiting** opens on day one with a three-step guide: scout, offer, visit. Each recruit's card says what he cares
  about and, once he's leaning elsewhere, why he says no ("Says no: Kentucky leads by 1"). Signing day is in spring.
- **The first game** comes about three weeks in. With **Watch games** ticked, game day opens on the HSN studio show (or
  tick **Skip the show**), then the full-screen live court with play-by-play, timeouts and shouts. The halftime desk and
  the postgame show follow. **Sim the game** jumps to the result.
- **Continue** plays one day; **Sim week** and **Next game** go further. A whole season to March takes under a minute
  headless.

## The season, at a glance
- **November to early March:** 29–32 regular-season games (non-conference first, then your conference), with the Top 25
  every Monday and the conference tournament in Championship Week.
- **The bubble (March Madness page, before Selection Sunday):** bracketology shows each bubble team's NET rank and
  Quad 1 record, and your own NET with the record in all four quadrants, as the committee reads a résumé.
- **Selection Sunday and March Madness:** your seed arrives in the inbox; the studio and the arena name the round.
  Left out? The 32 best teams left out play the **NIT**: the better seed hosts the first three rounds, and the
  semi-finals and the final are in Indianapolis.
- **The All-American games:** on February 1 the 24 best high-school seniors are named for the Hardwood All-American
  Classic (March 31, Chicago); the Monday after the Elite Eight the 20 best seniors outside the Final Four are named for
  the College All-American Game (the Friday before the semi-finals). East plays West; your picks, signees and
  recruits on your board are in your inbox, and the MVPs are on the Awards page and in the players' honours.
- **April to the summer:** the AD's season review (the verdict and the program's prestige move), your seniors' draft
  night, graduation, the new class enrolling, and the next season's expectations in October.
- **The coaching carousel (May):** after the reviews, 40–70 programs change coach (let go, retired, or hired away by a
  bigger program, which opens that job in turn). One inbox story lists your conference's and rival's changes first;
  the Board page has the whole spring. A bigger job that opens may come to you if you beat your goal.
- **Your non-conference schedule (Board page, any time before the summer ends):** let the conference office draw it,
  book 5 buy games at home (the AD expects one more win) or sign 3 home-and-home series with power programs (the AD
  allows one win; the return games come the next season); pick a holiday tournament (the three biggest invite B
  programs or better) and ask for a December showcase. The season's welcome names your slate and strength of schedule.
- **Zones and the press (Tactics page, and the live game's Tactics tab):** besides man-to-man and the 2-3, college
  benches play the 1-3-1 (traps the wings, forces turnovers, leaves the corners and the glass open) and the match-up
  zone, and can press full court after every basket (about 3 more turnovers a game against it, a quicker game, but a
  broken press gives up layups and your players tire faster). About one program in three plays a zone and one in nine
  presses; the scouting report warns you about a pressing opponent.
- **No transfers and no redshirts:** a player stays at the program he enrolls at for four years (Danny's rule).

## Known issues (not worth a note unless they bother you)
- **Blue bloods recruit too well** for years in a row, and prestige moves slowly after a bad season (K21, K27).
- **Big conferences play 19 league games** (real ones play 18 or 20; H197, after launch).
- **Sound:** the release zip carries the licensed pack: the arena crowd, the court sounds (dribbles, squeaks, the rim and the net) and the band's drumline and brass stabs. Every college home game also plays a fight song for the school (the real one where it's public domain, an original tune otherwise) on a simple band made in code. Nobody has tuned it by ear yet, and players' shouts and a crowd "aww" are still missing. If a game has no dribbles or drumline, that zip was built without the pack: leave a note. Your "restaurant" note stays open until it sounds right to you.
- National teams, FIBA and the Olympics aren't built yet; international play starts after the college plan.

## How to leave notes
One note per thing in the notebook, tagged **Bored**, **Confused**, **Kept waiting**, **Felt wrong**, **Bug** or
**Liked it**, with the page and the game date from the top bar. Say what you expected and what happened. A **Bug** note
becomes a finding the next run fixes first; the rest decide the order of work after launch.

## Saving
The career saves every 7 days, at each phase change, after your decisions, on Settings' **Save now**, and when you go to
the main menu or close the window. The top bar shows the last save under the date. Quit and load once in the first
session: it should come back on the day you left, and the next days should play out the same.
