# Start here: your first college career

This is the short guide for Danny's first college play-through: the launch build, "Your first college career"
(v0.194.0, Thu Nov 26, 2026), with the whole college plan in it. It replaces the old NBA play-test guide (v0.112.1). Notes go in the play-test notebook:
https://claude.ai/artifact/9MjakW3Aqhy7hqiZgrHF3r. The next build run reads them first.

## Before you start
- Download the newest release zip from the GitHub releases page (`HardwoodManager-vX.Y.Z-win-x64.zip`), unzip it
  anywhere and run `HardwoodManager.exe`. Any later release loads your save, so you can update whenever a new one lands.
- Your graphics packs (`prographics`, `collegegraphics` in `Desktop\FMNEWGANv2`) load at start as before.
- Saves live in `Documents\Hardwood Manager`. Keep the file: every later build is tested against a launch-build save.

## Starting the career (two minutes)
1. On the start screen, set **League** (top right) to **College basketball**.
2. Pick your program in the list. Blue bloods (Duke, North Carolina, Kentucky) start near the top; a mid-major is a
   rebuild with a patient AD.
3. Fill in your coach on the left (name, age, background, licence). Leave the seed as it is or press Random.
4. Press **Start career with selected team**.

## What to expect in the first hour
- **The Welcome** is the first inbox message: your program, the AD, the season goal, your rival, the first game and how
  recruiting works. The **assistant** panel on the right lists what needs you before you play on.
- **Recruiting** opens on day one with a three-step guide: scout, offer, visit. Each recruit's card says what he cares
  about and, once he's leaning elsewhere, why he says no ("Says no: Kentucky leads by 1"). Signing day is in spring.
- **The first game** comes about three weeks in. With **Watch games** ticked, game day opens on the HSN studio show (or
  tick **Skip the show**), then the full-screen live court with play-by-play, timeouts and shouts. The halftime desk and
  the postgame show follow. **Sim the game** jumps to the result.
- **Continue** plays one day; **Sim week** and **Next game** go further. A whole season to March takes under a minute
  headless.

## The season, at a glance
- **November to early March:** 29–32 regular-season games (non-conference first, then your conference), with the Top 25
  every Monday and the conference tournament in Championship Week.
- **The bubble (March Madness page, before Selection Sunday):** bracketology shows each bubble team's NET rank and
  Quad 1 record, and your own NET with the record in all four quadrants, as the committee reads a résumé.
- **Selection Sunday and March Madness, on the real weekdays:** conference tournaments run Friday to Sunday, with the
  finals on Selection Sunday (three weeks before the Final Four). The First Four is Tuesday and Wednesday, the first two rounds
  Thursday to Sunday, the Sweet 16 and Elite Eight the next Thursday to Sunday (each half of the bracket on its own two
  days, so nobody plays two days running), the Final Four the first Saturday of April and the final on Monday. Your
  seed arrives in the inbox; the studio and the arena name the round. The bracket page shows the whole field.
  Left out? The 32 best teams left out play the **NIT**: the better seed hosts the first three rounds, and the
  semi-finals and the final are in Indianapolis.
- **Captains (a week before the first game):** the players name two captains, the ones the room follows (leadership
  first, then seniority). Their leadership sets the team's chemistry; a poor pick or an unhappy captain costs it. Change
  them on the Squad page (pick a player, then Make captain), but a player who loses the captaincy takes it badly.
  Senior Night lifts your seniors for the rest of the season, and on the night of the national final the inbox says
  farewell to each of them.
- **The All-American games:** on February 1 the 24 best high-school seniors are named for the Hardwood All-American
  Classic (March 31, Chicago); the Monday after the Elite Eight the 20 best seniors outside the Final Four are named for
  the College All-American Game (the Friday before the semi-finals). East plays West; your picks, signees and
  recruits on your board are in your inbox, and the MVPs are on the Awards page and in the players' honours.
- **April to the summer:** the AD's season review (the verdict and the program's prestige move), your seniors' draft
  night, graduation, the new class enrolling, and the next season's expectations in October.
- **The coaching carousel (May):** after the reviews, 40–70 programs change coach (let go, retired, or hired away by a
  bigger program, which opens that job in turn). One inbox story lists your conference's and rival's changes first;
  the Board page has the whole spring. A bigger job that opens may come to you if you beat your goal.
- **Your non-conference schedule (Board page, any time before the summer ends):** let the conference office draw it,
  book 5 buy games at home (the AD expects one more win) or sign 3 home-and-home series with power programs (the AD
  allows one win; the return games come the next season); pick a holiday tournament (the three biggest invite B
  programs or better) and ask for a December showcase. The season's welcome names your slate and strength of schedule.
- **Zones and the press (Tactics page, and the live game's Tactics tab):** besides man-to-man and the 2-3, college
  benches play the 1-3-1 (traps the wings, forces turnovers, leaves the corners and the glass open) and the match-up
  zone, and can press full court after every basket (about 3 more turnovers a game against it, a quicker game, but a
  broken press gives up layups and your players tire faster). About one program in three plays a zone and one in nine
  presses; the scouting report warns you about a pressing opponent.
- **Program legacy (the team page's Legacy tab):** every program keeps a record book (career and single-season
  leaders, its best team seasons). On the night of each national final the seniors' careers close: the best go into
  the program's Hall of Fame, and the greatest have their jerseys retired. The inbox and the news tell you about yours.
- **Rival coaches (Home and the Board page):** every other head coach is a person with a style (recruiter, tactician,
  defensive mind, player developer, motivator, up-tempo), a record, bids, titles and a reputation. Home names tonight's
  opposing coach, the Board page lists your rivals' coaches, and they speak after a rivalry game and when they beat you
  to a recruit. Their styles are colour, not a hidden edge.
- **Your coaching career and coaching tree (the Board page's "Your coaching career" panel):** every season you coach
  is booked: record, seed and how far you went in March. Milestones (your first win, every 100 wins, your first bid,
  tournament win, Final Four and title, 10 seasons) come once each in the inbox and the news. After two seasons with
  you, a good assistant may be hired away as a head coach in the carousel: that's your coaching tree. A long, winning
  stay with bids (or a title in a stay of five seasons or more) puts a coach in that program's Hall, and that includes you.
- **Player stories over four years (the inbox, the Roster and the player page):** every player has an origin (his
  home state, his stars and his rank as a recruit). Your own players' stories are told once each, from real game data:
  the freshman phenom, the hometown hero, the walk-on who earns minutes (and a scholarship the next summer if one is
  open), the senior's breakout and the one the big programs overlooked.
- **The columnists (new in v0.209.0, the inbox):** three made-up writers cover your career: your local beat writer, a national
  college writer (Dana Whitcombe) and a stats writer (Priya Venkataraman); in a pro career an old-school columnist (Walt
  Brennecke) takes the college writer's place. Their preseason picks of your conference ride in the preseason message; on
  Selection Sunday they are judged against the final table ("picked you 12th. You finished 9th."). In a pro career the picks
  arrive on day one, the stats writer's power rankings ride in about every fourth weekly results message, and the verdict
  comes when the playoffs start.
- **No transfers and no redshirts:** a player stays at the program he enrolls at for four years (Danny's rule).
- **International (new in v0.195.0, the left menu):** every country with twelve players aged 17+ anywhere in the world
  fields a national team (about 40), ranked in the FIBA world ranking. Pick one to see its twelve and their clubs; the FIBA
  calendar shows the windows, the 2027 World Cup in Qatar and the 2028 Olympics in Los Angeles. **From v0.197.0 the 2027
  World Cup is played** (32 teams, groups, a second round and the knockouts, every game in the full engine on FIBA rules):
  it's played at the summer's jump to the new season, the inbox tells you who won gold, and the page shows the medals,
  the knockouts, the group tables, the top scorers and an all-star five (`claude/international-plan.md`). College rosters are all American, so your players can only
  be in the USA's pool. **From v0.198.0 the qualifying windows are played** (late November and late February, quick
  results; each zone in groups of about four, two games a window): the inbox gets one message a window, the page shows
  the cycle's group tables, and the group winners and runners-up take their zone's World Cup places. The ranking
  starts from the real FIBA order blended with each twelve's strength, then moves with every window and World Cup game.
  **From v0.199.0 the 2028 Olympics and the 2029 continental championships are played** at the summer's jump: four
  Olympic qualifying tournaments of six (quick results) in early July, then twelve teams in Los Angeles (the USA as hosts,
  the World Cup's best per zone and the four qualifiers; three groups, the quarter-finals to the final, the full engine);
  and in 2029 EuroBasket, the AmeriCup, AfroBasket and the Asia Cup, with fields from the continental qualifying tables
  (as many teams as each zone has, up to 24, 12, 16 and 16). The page's "Champions and medals" panel lists every
  tournament played and the World Cup and Olympic medal table.
  **From v0.201.0 call-ups count:** a player picked for (or left out of) a tournament comes back happier (or not), injuries
  picked up there carry to his club, and from 2030 there's a U23 Nations Cup. **From v0.202.0 you can coach a nation:**
  federations call from Oct 15 by your reputation (smaller nations first); accept on the International page and pick your
  twelve there. **From v0.203.0 you coach its qualifiers live** on days your club is idle (your assistant takes them on club
  days); results move your reputation, and a tournament far below the seeding ends the job. **From v0.204.0** NBA clubs
  don't release players for the November and February windows (they play the summer events), and NBA and national games no
  longer have the college band.
- **From v0.205.0 the clubs abroad are real** in Spain, Turkey, Greece, Italy, France, Germany and Lithuania: Real Madrid,
  Fenerbahçe, Panathinaikos, Olimpia Milano, Monaco, Alba Berlin, Zalgiris Kaunas and the rest, in their cities, with their
  arenas (the Team page's Arena chip) and real capacities, and the giants are strong in a new career. Every league abroad has
  its real name (Liga ACB, the EuroLeague, the EuroCup, the B.League …). The rosters are still generated. An older save gets
  the real names when it loads (its rosters stay as they were).
- **From v0.206.0 eight more leagues have their real clubs:** the NBL (Sydney Kings, Perth Wildcats, the Breakers), the CBA
  (Guangdong Southern Tigers, Liaoning Flying Leopards), the B.League (Alvark Tokyo, Ryukyu Golden Kings), the KBL, the PBA
  (San Miguel, Ginebra and TNT, all at the Araneta Coliseum), the Israeli league (Maccabi and Hapoel Tel Aviv), the ABA League
  (Crvena zvezda, Partizan) and the VTB League (CSKA, Zenit, UNICS). Poland, the Baltic, Czech, BNXT, the Americas and Africa
  are still made up (next build).
- **From v0.210.0 the European cups play their real formats.** The EuroLeague is 20 clubs: its licensed clubs (Real Madrid,
  Barcelona, Fenerbahçe, Olympiacos, Panathinaikos, Partizan, Zalgiris and the rest) enter every season whatever their league
  finish, and the best of the rest takes the last place. Home and away, 38 rounds from late September to mid-April (some weeks
  have two rounds), then a play-in for 7th-10th, best-of-five quarter-finals and the Final Four in late May. The EuroCup is 20
  clubs, everyone once, then single-game knockouts for the top 16 and a best-of-three final. The league page's Rules tab says
  how it works.

## Known issues (not worth a note unless they bother you)
- **The live court is smallish,** with a busy crowd around it, and in similar kits (white against light blue) the two
  teams' dots can be hard to tell apart (K36).
- **Unhappy players:** from v0.196.0 a repeated "X wants a word" is one inbox thread that says how long it has gone on
  ("X still wants a word"), and "Hire an assistant coach" shows on two days a phase. The mood itself still lingers (K37).
- **Timeouts:** from v0.196.0 college games have media timeouts (under 16, 12, 8 and 4 minutes of each half, "MEDIA
  TIMEOUT" in the commentary), and nobody calls one in the last two minutes of a 15-point game (K38).
- **Blue bloods recruit well** for years in a row, and prestige moves slowly after a bad season (K21, K27).
- **Big conferences play 19 league games** (real ones play 18 or 20; H197, after launch).
- **Sound:** the release zip carries the licensed pack: the arena crowd, the court sounds (dribbles, squeaks, the rim and the net) and the band's drumline and brass stabs (college games only; the pros have no PA or organ yet). Every college home game also plays a fight song for the school (the real one where it's public domain, an original tune otherwise) on a simple band made in code. Nobody has tuned it by ear yet, and players' shouts and a crowd "aww" are still missing. If a game has no dribbles or drumline, that zip was built without the pack: leave a note. Your "restaurant" note stays open until it sounds right to you.
- **Your nation's summer tournaments** are still played at the summer's jump, not live (K41). A club game and a national game on the same day leave the national game to your assistant. International games play at FIBA tournament pace, about 80 points a side (v0.200.0).

## How to leave notes
One note per thing in the notebook, tagged **Bored**, **Confused**, **Kept waiting**, **Felt wrong**, **Bug** or
**Liked it**, with the page and the game date from the top bar. Say what you expected and what happened. A **Bug** note
becomes a finding the next run fixes first; the rest decide the order of work after launch.

## Saving
The career saves every 7 days, at each phase change, after your decisions, on Settings' **Save now**, and when you go to
the main menu or close the window. The top bar shows the last save under the date. Quit and load once in the first
session: it should come back on the day you left, and the next days should play out the same.
